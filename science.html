What Is Dragon's Blood?

Dragon's blood isn't a marketing name. It's the traditional name for the deep red resin tapped from the bark of a handful of remarkable trees - from Croton lechleri in the Amazon rainforest and Daemonorops draco in Southeast Asia to the Dracaena dragon trees of Asia. For centuries, communities have applied the sap directly to cuts, burns and irritated skin to protect it and speed healing - and today, dermatology and cosmetic science are catching up with what traditional medicine already knew.

Dragon blood trees belong to Dracaena - a genus of 60 to 100 species, of which only a handful grow into true trees. They are among the most distinctive plants on Earth: a stout trunk crowned by a dense, upturned umbrella of stiff, sword-like leaves.

Cut the bark, and the tree appears to bleed. A deep crimson resin seeps from the wound - the legendary dragon's blood that gives these trees their name and has been prized for thousands of years.

Our serum is based on the species found in southern China - Dracaena cochinchinensis, the Chinese dragon blood tree. It grows on limestone hills across Yunnan and neighbouring regions, and its resin (known as lóng xuè jié, "dragon's blood") has centuries of history in traditional Chinese practice.

Where Our Trees Grow

Our dragon blood trees come from Yunnan province in southern China - a high, sun-drenched region of limestone hills and red-earth plateaus on the edge of the Himalayas.

Yunnan is one of the most botanically rich places on Earth, and it has a deep connection to this tree. Near Kunming, on the famous red earth of Dongchuan, stands a revered ancient dragon tree believed to be around a thousand years old - honoured by local villagers as a symbol of vitality and protection, and now under formal judicial protection.

It's this landscape - and this species, Dracaena cochinchinensis - that our dragon blood extract is based on.

Built for Survival

Dragon blood trees are monocots - botanical cousins of palms and grasses - yet unlike almost all of their relatives, their trunks thicken year after year through a rare form of secondary growth.

Every branch splits into two identical limbs, a pattern called dichotomous branching. Repeated over centuries, it produces the tree's iconic dome: a tight lattice of branches tipped with rosettes of leathery, water-conserving leaves.

That umbrella crown isn't just beautiful. In the arid, high-altitude places these trees call home, it shades the roots, captures moisture from mist and cloud, and lets the species thrive where few other trees survive at all. It is this hardiness - a metabolism built around protection and repair - that makes the tree's resin so interesting for skin.

The Crimson Resin

When the bark is wounded, the tree seals the cut with a rich red resin - its own natural repair system, tapped for millennia without felling the tree. This is not a trend ingredient: what's changed isn't the resin, it's our ability to isolate and study the compounds responsible for what it does.

What's Actually in It

The resin isn't a single "active" - it's a concentrated mix of plant compounds, several of which have documented effects on skin.

ProanthocyanidinsThe majority of the resin's dry weight. Potent antioxidants that help neutralise the free radicals generated by UV exposure, pollution and everyday environmental stress - the kind of damage that shows up over time as fine lines, dullness and uneven tone.
TaspineAn alkaloid unique to the resin, and the compound most associated with its wound-healing reputation. Research points to taspine supporting fibroblast activity - the skin cells responsible for repairing tissue.
Phenols & flavonoidsA broader phenolic profile rounds out the resin, contributing additional anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activity alongside the proanthocyanidins.

What This Means for Your Skin Barrier

Your skin's barrier is the outermost layer that keeps moisture in and irritants out. When it's compromised - by over-exfoliation, harsh actives, weather, or simply age - skin gets reactive, dehydrated and dull faster.

Dragon's blood extract is studied for its ability to support that barrier function directly: helping reduce water loss through the skin, calming visible redness and irritation, and giving skin the conditions it needs to repair itself.

That's why it works as a daily layer rather than an occasional treatment - it supports a process instead of masking a symptom.

Gentle by Nature

Unlike retinoids or high-strength acids, dragon's blood extract is considered a gentle ingredient with a low irritation profile - which is part of why it suits sensitive, reactive and compromised skin as well as normal skin. As with any new ingredient, patch test before full use, particularly if you have a latex sensitivity, since the resin shares some botanical similarity with natural latex.
